The "Research" page collects documents related to the kind of space and equipment we use. We publish them for transparency, not as a sales argument. To make the page useful, every document carries a label that says what kind of evidence it is. Here is what the labels mean and how we read them.

## The labels, from strongest to weakest

- **Peer-reviewed.** The article was read and commented on by other researchers before publication. It is a quality filter, not a guarantee that the results are correct or apply to other situations.
- **Clinical study.** Research with a protocol, usually a comparison group and a public registration. We use this label only when the document really has those elements.
- **Pilot study.** A small piece of research done to see whether a question deserves a larger study. It does not demonstrate effects and cannot be generalised.
- **Case report.** A description of one person's experience. Interesting as a starting point, impossible to generalise.
- **Review.** A summary of other studies. Its value depends on the quality of the studies included and the selection criteria.
- **Anecdotal report.** Subjective accounts with no comparison group. Not scientific evidence; we show them as additional material only.
- **Other.** Technical documents, presentations, manufacturer material.

## Three questions before drawing a conclusion

1. **Who took part, and how many?** Ten people and two thousand people tell very different stories.
2. **What was it compared with?** Without a comparison group, any change may have other explanations: time, rest, expectations.
3. **What do the authors say under "limitations"?** Serious authors describe their own limits. If that section is missing, read even more carefully.

## What the library does not mean

A document being in the library does not mean our sessions produce the effects described in it. We do not claim or imply that sessions have any effect on any condition. Sessions are relaxation experiences; for any health question, talk to your doctor.

## How we maintain the list

Each document appears once, with authors, year, source and evidence type. When we receive a new document, we label it by its actual content, not its title.
